Terrarium — You’ve just taken control of a nation’s economy in 1946, and here’s the catch: you can’t even see it clearly. Your GDP figures are late, noisy, and will be quietly revised long after you’ve bet your political career on them. In this browser-based strategy game, you steer a country through a century of quarterly decisions — taxes, rates, reforms — inside a small but genuinely alive economy where a fuel tax ripples through transport into bread prices without a single scripted rule. The obvious moves backfire for the same reasons they backfire in reality: print money to fund a UBI your tax base can’t support and inflation eats it; force industrialization and watch your elites strangle the innovation that would make you rich. Every lever is available from turn one — the game never tells you no, it just lets you find out. Build statistical agencies to lift the fog, spend crises to force reforms your enemies would otherwise veto, and try to keep your nation inside the narrow corridor between despotism and anarchy where prosperity actually lives. It’s Democracy’s clarity wired to Victoria’s emergent guts — a game where getting smarter about economics is the power fantasy.
